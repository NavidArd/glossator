# Conservation laws

Every continuous symmetry of a physical system comes with a quantity that does not change in time. Invariance under shifts in time gives energy; invariance under shifts in space gives momentum; invariance under rotations gives angular momentum.

The deep reason is that the action, the integral of the Lagrangian over time, is unchanged by the symmetry. Varying the path along the symmetry direction then produces a total time derivative, and on a solution of the equations of motion that derivative must vanish.

This pattern repeats across physics. Gauge symmetry in electromagnetism gives conservation of electric charge. Internal symmetries of the strong interaction give conserved quantum numbers such as baryon number, at least approximately.

Symmetries can also be broken. When the laws are symmetric but the state is not, as with a magnet that picks one direction, the symmetry is said to be spontaneously broken, and new low-energy excitations appear.
